Threat Library

Know Your Enemy

Phishing comes in many forms. Understanding each type is your first line of defense.

📧
Email Phishing

Fake emails impersonating banks, PayPal, or tech companies. They urge you to click a link and "verify" your account.

🔍 How to Spot It
Check the sender's actual email address — it's never the real domain.
📱
Smishing (SMS)

Phishing attacks delivered over text messages — fake parcel tracking, bank alerts, or OTP requests with malicious links.

🔍 How to Spot It
Legit services never ask for passwords over SMS. Never click unknown links in texts.
📞
Vishing (Voice)

Scammers call pretending to be tech support, the IRS, or your bank — creating urgency to extract credentials or money.

🔍 How to Spot It
Hang up and call the official number yourself. Real agents never ask for OTPs.
🎯
Spear Phishing

Highly targeted attacks using your personal info (name, employer, colleagues) to craft convincing, personalized messages.

🔍 How to Spot It
Even if an email "knows" you, verify through a separate channel before acting.
👑
Whaling

CEO fraud — targeting executives with fake urgent requests: wire transfers, credential resets, or confidential data leaks.

🔍 How to Spot It
Always verify financial requests via phone, even if the email looks legitimate.
🌐
Pharming

DNS poisoning that redirects you from a real website to a fake one — without you ever clicking a suspicious link.

🔍 How to Spot It
Check the padlock and exact domain in your browser bar on every sensitive site.

Red Flags in Suspicious URLs

Learn to read a URL like a security expert. These patterns almost always signal danger.

⚠️ Example Phishing URL Breakdown

http :// secure-login . paypa1 . verify-account . xyz / login?redirect=bank
🔴 No HTTPS 🔴 Hyphens in domain 🔴 Typo: "paypa1" (digit not L) 🔴 Suspicious TLD (.xyz) 🔴 Keyword stuffing
🔒
No HTTPS
Real banks and shops always use HTTPS. HTTP means no encryption — your data is exposed.
🔤
Typosquatting
Subtle misspellings like "paypa1.com" or "arnazon.com" trick the eye at a glance.
➖
Excessive Hyphens
"secure-login-paypal-verify.com" — real companies rarely use hyphens in their primary domain.
🌐
Suspicious TLDs
Domains ending in .xyz, .biz, .info, .club are dirt-cheap and heavily abused by phishers.
🔢
IP Address as Domain
"http://192.168.1.1/login" — legitimate websites use domain names, not raw IP addresses.
📏
Abnormally Long URL
Phishing URLs often stuff keywords and parameters to look official while hiding the real domain.
Protection

Stay One Step Ahead

Six habits that will make you almost impossible to phish.

🔍
Verify Before You Click

Hover over links to preview the real URL. When in doubt, go directly to the official website by typing it yourself.

🔐
Enable 2FA Everywhere

Two-factor authentication means attackers can't log in even if they steal your password. Use an authenticator app, not SMS.

🗝️
Use a Password Manager

Password managers auto-fill only on the correct domain — if the URL is fake, they won't fill in your credentials.

📬
Don't Trust Urgency

"Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!" is a classic fear tactic. Slow down — urgency is a manipulation tool.

🔄
Keep Software Updated

Phishing often exploits outdated browsers or plugins. Enable auto-updates and use a browser with built-in phishing protection.

🚨
Report Phishing Attempts

Forward suspicious emails to your IT team or report@phishing.gov. Reporting helps protect thousands of others from the same attack.
